Edytor's collaboration stack is proven by its test suites across Chromium, Firefox and WebKit, but it has no production track record yet. This page lists what is out of scope, what is not built, and the edge cases you may meet; [Platform support](/docs/reference/platform-support) lists the browsers, devices and input methods the suites run, and what they do not (real phones, operating-system IMEs). The screen-reader passes the [accessibility page](/docs/editor/accessibility) lists are not recorded yet.

## Not provided

- **No hosted infrastructure.** Edytor ships the [Durable Object room](/docs/server/quick-start); you deploy and operate it on your own Cloudflare account. There is no edytor cloud.
- **No authentication or permission rules.** No accounts, sessions, sharing model or roles. The room calls your [`authorize`](/docs/server/authorization) and enforces what it returns (user, replica, read-only), nothing more.
- **No backups or document management.** The room stores one document in its Durable Object's SQLite storage. Listing, exporting, backing up and deleting documents are up to your application (`document.encode()` and `facade.toJSON()` give you the content). The room's [version history](/docs/server/history) keeps two versions a day for 30 days, in the store you choose (KV, R2, the room's own storage or one of your own): it is not a backup. Versions kept in the room's own storage go with its Durable Object, and a version larger than its store's value limit is skipped.
- **IndexedDB is not durability.** The local copy lives in the browser and can be cleared by the user or the browser. Durable storage is the room's.

## Not finished

- **Simple tables only.** The [table plugin](/docs/plugins/table) is Notion's simple table: cells of text, no merged cells, no cell selection, no sorting, filters or formulas, and no database views. A text range across cells deletes each cell's covered text; copy of a part of a table copies its text.
- **Comments are plain text.** The [comments plugin](/docs/plugins/comments) keeps threads on text ranges with replies, resolve and reopen, stored by the room. A comment has no formatting, mentions or attachments, cannot be edited once posted, and anchors to text only (not to a block); there is no comment-only access level: a read-only identity can read threads but not write them.
- **Version history is two versions a day.** The room keeps [two versions a day](/docs/server/history), and the [history panel](/docs/server/history-panel) lists, previews (with the blocks added, removed and changed since) and restores them; there is no per-edit timeline (see History and purge below).
- **Suggestions stay in one view.** A [suggestion](/docs/editor/suggestions) (text, blocks or a rewrite proposed by your AI) shows only in the view that made it until it is accepted: another tab or a collaborator cannot review it, and a reload loses it.
- **No property schema.** [Properties](/docs/concepts/properties) take any JSON; validate or normalize in `onBeforeOperation`.
- **Columns have no colours of their own.** A [columns layout](/docs/plugins/columns#resizing) resizes with the pointer or, on a focused gap, with the arrow keys.
- **Array items that are strings or numbers have no key.** An object item's proxy follows its item, so a keyed `{#each}` row stays on it; a row bound to a string item by index (`bind:value={tags[i]}`) writes whatever item is at that index when a collaborator inserts or removes one above it. Store objects for rows several people edit.

## Localization

The chrome's words (menus, toolbar, handles, panels, placeholders, announcements) come from a [labels dictionary](/docs/customization/localization), English by default: each plugin takes its `labels`, and `<Edytor labels>` the view's own. Edytor ships no translation of its own: write the dictionary for your language, typed as `Labels`, so a release that adds a label fails your build until you translate it.

- **Your own kinds, marks, commands and groups** are named by what you write in their records.
- **Mac key symbols** (⌘, ⇧, ↵) show on a Mac whatever the labels.
- **The chrome is placed for a left-to-right page.** Blocks take their own text's direction ([Right-to-left text](/docs/editor/accessibility#right-to-left-text)); the menus, toolbar and handles keep their left-to-right layout and placement in a right-to-left page.

## Mixed-version peers

- Clients of different document generations never exchange edits: providers and the room refuse the other's frames and report `'protocol-mismatch'` or `'schema-mismatch'`. Deploy one version to every client.
- Older clients of the **same** generation ignore the per-person delete and undo records. They show a block whose creation was undone (empty, or with another person's text); their own undo still deletes a block outright; and a text deleted by two people and undone by both can appear twice on them. A current client may also bring back text it held back after an old client deleted it, since an old client's delete carries no record.
- Clients from before properties were stored per leaf read a block's or inline block's `data` only from its old whole-value attribute: after a current client writes a property there, they show the value from before it, and they do not see the document's properties. Clients from before array items were stored per item read an array a current client wrote as an object keyed by its items' ids.
- Clients of an earlier pre-release have no layout role: they show a stored [columns layout](/docs/plugins/columns) as unknown kinds, its blocks one under the other, and apply none of its rules (an empty column or a one-column layout a current client would hide shows there). A current view without `columnsPlugin` shows a layout the same way. Deploy the plugin to every view before your users make layouts.
- Clients that predate the saved and chunk messages report them through `'message-error'` and otherwise sync small documents normally.
- A server that never sends saved acknowledgements leaves the provider's `unsaved` count growing; one that acknowledges only state vectors leaves delete-only updates unsaved.

## Offline first visit

A device whose first visit to a document happens offline seeds the `value` you passed (after the readiness bound) and keeps that seed. When it reconnects, the seed merges into the room's content. Seeds are deterministic, so pass the same `value` everywhere (or none) and equal seeds merge into one; a different `value` adds its blocks next to the room's. To avoid it, set [`requireHydration`](/docs/collaboration/documents#require-hydration): the view stays read-only and seeds nothing until the document was fetched once. To have documents ready before a device goes offline, [`prefetch`](/docs/collaboration/persistence#prefetch-and-last-updated) them.

The WebSocket's bound starts when the socket opens (or first fails to), so a room that is slow to accept the connection is waited for, up to the connect timeout. Two cases remain:

- A server that opens the socket but takes more than 1 s to send its state is treated like an empty room: the `value` is seeded and later merges as above.
- A dial that neither opens nor fails (a connection the network silently drops, or a room still loading) is given up after `connectTimeout` (10 s by default), so an empty document waits up to 11 s before it seeds. For rooms whose load can take longer, raise `connectTimeout` in `createWebsocketSync`, or set it to `Infinity` to wait as long as the browser does.

## Known edge cases

- **Undo of text deleted twice.** When two people delete the same text, it comes back only when both undo. If one of them has closed their session, their replica no longer hides a concurrent restoration, although their delete still holds back later undos.
- **Text typed and deleted within one undo step** leaves nothing to restore.
- **Two splits at the same position.** Both new lines exist; the text goes to one and the other stays empty.
- **Line order in some races.** A new line added beside a block (Enter at its start or end, Duplicate, +, a paste of whole blocks or over selected blocks), several structural edits by one person before a sync, and concurrent moves can order lines by client id instead of by the text. See [which races keep the text order](/docs/collaboration/concurrent-editing#which-races-keep-the-text-order).
- **Undoing a split** joins the halves again, even if someone typed in the second half. See [concurrent editing](/docs/collaboration/concurrent-editing#undoing-a-new-block-keeps-a-peers-text).
- **Contributors are block lineage, not authorship.** `attribution.block(id).contributors` lists who edited that block id (through splits and merges too), not who wrote the text shown now.
- **Your own `UndoManager`.** An undo manager created directly with `new Y.UndoManager` over the document does not follow edytor's undo rules. Use `document.history`.
- **A read-only view syncs read-only.** A `readonly` view with `room` and `server` (or `sync`) connects and shows peers' edits live, and writes nothing; the room decides what an identity may write (`readOnly` in `authorize`).
- **`saved` after a reload.** The provider counts what the document's actor wrote, recognized by the client ids the document binds to that actor. Without an `actor`, the anonymous id changes every session, so edits made before a reload no longer count in `unsaved`. A delete restored from the local copy counts only when its update wrote no other author's content, since a delete does not record who made it.
- **Columns: a selection dragged over the bare gap.** Dragging a text selection from column 1 into the gap between two columns never selects column 2, but past the gap's middle (over the gap itself, not a block's handle) it extends to the end of column 1.
- **Columns: <kbd>Escape</kbd> from the block menu** opened on a block in a column gives back a caret, not the block selection.
- **Columns: native <kbd>↑</kbd>/<kbd>↓</kbd>** move visually and differ per engine (Firefox from column 1's last line goes below the layout, Chromium and WebKit into column 2); see [Columns](/docs/plugins/columns#keys).
- **One user per browser profile.** The local copy and the cross-tab channel are shared by every tab of a profile; see [persistence](/docs/collaboration/persistence#one-user-per-browser-profile).

## Size and platform limits

- **A document's stored size grows with every edit.** About 72 bytes an edit, for the document's life: the CRDT structure each edit leaves, which the [purge](/docs/server/history#the-purge) barely reduces. A document many people edit every day reaches the 2 MiB default in days to weeks; past the quota the room refuses writes (`4413`) and the provider stops, its later edits unsaved. The maximum document size is yours to choose: raise `EDYTOR_MAX_DOCUMENT_BYTES` as far as the room's memory allows (below), and watch the room's `size` log, written when a document reaches 80 % of it (`EDYTOR_DOCUMENT_WARNING`). Re-seeding a document from its content, which would shrink it, is not part of 1.0: every client's local copy would have to start over, and edits made offline meanwhile would not merge.
- **Long pages type slower.** The editor renders every block, with no windowing, and in Chromium each keystroke also costs time proportional to the page. Keep a document to a couple of thousand top-level blocks for typing within a frame; see [Long pages](/docs/customization/styling#long-pages).
- A room document is capped at 2 MiB stored by default (about 5,000 paragraphs of prose): the room holds the live document in an isolate of 128 MB, where it takes 18 to 46 times its stored size. Raise [the quota](/docs/server/room#quotas) for documents of long text; split very long lists of short blocks across documents. A single WebSocket message is capped at 32 MiB by Cloudflare; both the provider and the room send larger frames as chunks. The image plugin refuses an inline (`data:`) image over 1 MiB: [upload](/docs/plugins/image) it instead.
- Document ids and user ids are 1 to 256 characters, and a user id with a lone surrogate is refused `4403` (see [authorize](/docs/server/authorization#authorize)). A document id may hold any character: the provider percent-encodes it into one path segment, and your Worker decodes it (see [the quick start](/docs/server/quick-start)). The ids `.` and `..` are the exception, and so is an id with a lone surrogate: a URL collapses those segments (`%2E` too) and cannot encode a lone surrogate, so the provider throws for them and `routeDocumentSocket` closes them `4400`.
- After a room wakes from hibernation, presence refills as clients renew it, within 15 seconds.
- Deleted content stays in the document so undo and offline peers keep working: a withdrawn block keeps its node (about 100 bytes), and each text delete adds about 12 bytes to the stored document. Compacting the room's rows stores the live document, which drops deleted text no one can restore, but keeps what undo and offline peers may still need.
- With `lineage` enabled, history entries trimmed from a block's ring leave small tombstones in storage.
- Deleted content is purged after 30 days ([`purgeAfterDays`](/docs/server/history#the-purge)). A purged block leaves its id behind (about 5 bytes stored, compressed). A deleted block a visible one still depends on keeps its bare structure (its id, kind, place and delete marks, about 25 bytes stored, compressed): a line split from a paragraph that is still there (Enter makes most lines so) shares that paragraph's text, and a block moved into a deleted one shows in its place. Its text and properties are purged all the same.
- A [version](/docs/server/history) over its store's value limit (KV's 25 MiB, R2's 128 MiB, the room's own 32 MiB) is skipped, not split; versions are two a day, and only one restore can be undone, until the next restore or the purge horizon.

## History and purge

- **Two versions a day, not every edit.** The history keeps the state at local noon and midnight of each half-day that changed; finer history is the per-block attribution and the undo history.
- **A restore undone revives with the room's delete.** A restore that brought back a block someone deleted, then undone, deletes it again as the room: that person's own undo of their delete no longer brings it back (restore a version instead).
- **An undo older than 30 days restores nothing.** Once the room's purge passed it, an undo history drops the step.
- **A device offline past the purge horizon** keeps its edits to content that still exists; its edits to content purged meanwhile (typed into a block deleted 30 days ago and more) are dropped, a block it moved under a purged block shows at the top level, and a split of such a block keeps no text. See [the purge](/docs/server/history#undo-and-clients-offline-for-long).
